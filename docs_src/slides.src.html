The problem

Nobody can tell an ED patient what the visit will cost

155.4MED visits in the U.S. in 2022 (CDC)
5×spread for one service: a level 4 visit is $321 to $1,712 across 23 insurers at Yale New Haven
724,913rows in the hospital's public price file, organized by billing code
28%of adults skipped or delayed care over cost in the past year (KFF, 2026)
  • The price is set after care: a visit level, plus every test that was ordered.
  • The legal right to a "good faith estimate" does not cover walk-in or same-day care.
  • So patients decide blind, and the bill arrives weeks later.
What is new

Start from symptoms, not billing codes

What exists today

  • Hospital and insurer estimators: you must already know which service you need.
  • FAIR Health, Turquoise: built around a procedure, or area averages.
  • Goodbill: only after the bill arrives.
  • A general AI model: asked the same price three times, it said $60, $107 and $78. The hospital's file says $119.

This app

  • You describe the problem in a sentence.
  • It predicts the likely ED workup.
  • It prices each line at this hospital's rate for your insurer.
  • It says what you pay: low, likely and high.
  • It covers "what if I'm admitted?"
  • It tells you who to ask what.
  • The AI cannot invent a number.
How it works

Four agents in a fixed line; code does the math

GreenAI agents, all on gpt-6-luna Sandplain code, zero tokens. The cheapest agent goes first and can end the run.

Did cutting cost hurt?

Not on what was measured: both settings picked the same scenario, codes, insurer and totals. The wording of summaries was not scored. Five cases is a small sample.

Safety

The hardest problem: a price next to an emergency

  • Care first. Warning signs trigger "call 911 or go now" in code, with three reassurances above the price. I tried hiding the price in emergencies and took it out: people have a right to know.
  • Attacks. Injection is refused in code, whether typed in or hidden in the data. No agent can read files or keys.
  • Made-up facts and runaway spend. Unknown codes are rejected, every dollar must match the pricing code, and runs are capped.

A colleague who tried it

Workup right? "Yes." What do you tell patients today? "Won't answer." Useful? "No… you don't want them to be influenced to seek emergency care based on pricing."

A patient who tried it

"Useful if I am budgeting AFTER my visit…" but "I want to know what… procedure codes and costs it looked at" and "The uncertainty is too big."

Both replies changed the app the same day: shorter results, then an itemized list of each number's source.
