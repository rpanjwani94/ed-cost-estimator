Care first. Cost can wait.

  • The hospital must treat you. In an emergency it has to examine and stabilize you whether or not you can pay or have insurance.
  • The bill can be lowered afterward. Hospitals have financial assistance and interest-free payment plans. You can ask after you have been seen.
  • Insurance has to cover emergencies. With most private plans, emergency care is billed at in-network rates even at an out-of-network hospital.

How it works

  1. Safety screenerchecks for warning signs that need care right away.
  2. Clinical plannerworks out the visit level, tests and scans an ED would likely order.
  3. Pricing analystprices that plan from the hospital's published rates for your insurer.
  4. Patient advocateexplains the estimate, your rights and what to ask.

Prices come from the hospital's public price transparency file. This is an estimate of the hospital's bill, not a quote, and not medical advice.
